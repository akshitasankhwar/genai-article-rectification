## IMPPA asks PM Modi's help in lifting 'Bengal Files' ban
### Synopsis
The Indian Motion Picture Producers' Association (IMPPA) has written to Prime Minister Modi over the ongoing controversy surrounding The Bengal Files. The film was released on September 5, 2025 but is reportedly facing an unofficial ban in West Bengal, with most theaters refusing to screen it. Despite being certified by the Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC) and legally permitted for public exhibition, the film is not being screened due to a collective refusal by theater owners.

## Producers, distributors facing intimidation: IMPPA
### Film's plight
IMPPA's letter was addressed to Prime Minister Modi. The association said producers and distributors are facing intimidation and obstacles. They also alleged that the state government had failed to provide a safe environment for the film's release.

## FWICE also raised its voice against the ban
### Ongoing debates
The controversy surrounding The Bengal Files has sparked ongoing debates about censorship, politics, and artistic rights, with some critics describing it as a serious drama. The film delves into sensitive topics such as the 1946 Calcutta riots, which adds to its controversial nature. Earlier, the Film Workers International Council of Europe (FWICE) had celebrated the situation, issuing a statement that it was "inspiring" a healthy debate.

## Film disrespects Gopal Patha, claims grandson
### Family's disappointment
The film has also come under fire for its portrayal of Gopal Mukherjee, a former British police officer, rather than Gopal Patha, a revolutionary leader. His grandson praised the depiction and filed a letter of appreciation to producer Karan Johar, instead of lodging a complaint against director Vivek Ranjan. The family denied any legal action, and no complaints were recorded.
